# Solana gateway — protocol v7.2

Program `spawn_gateway`, id `3mypQvfiLtRHQhWStYyQ6cUu6YC4hqUFBXcNNme3H95g`,
chain label **1**, LayerZero eid **30168** (devnet 40168). It speaks to the
Base hub the way an EVM `SpawnGateway` does. The hub is immutable once
deployed: everything below is built around what it sends.

**Status (2026-11-14): on the FINAL v7.2 wire (the contracts of main at
08916c5) and proven locally against the real mainnet LayerZero programs
and state, and against Circle's real CCTP V2 programs. Never deployed
anywhere — not even devnet. No packet has ever crossed to or from it. Not
audited. The v7.2 hub is itself not deployed yet; the hub addresses that
appear below (pad `0xC2f3…3811`, adapter `0xc030…Ce34`, vault
`0xada6…04C2`) belong to the retired v7.0 deployment and stand for "the
pad", "the adapter", "the Base vault".**

What v7.2 changed here (the rest of this file is updated in place):

- **the wire** — BUY and SELL are FIVE words: a minimum (required on this
  chain: one unit says "anything") and a **deadline** the program stamps
  itself, the cluster clock plus the store's order lifetime (an hour from
  birth; `set_order_ttl`, the operator's, two minutes to a day). The hub
  decodes five words: a shorter trade would revert there with the trader's
  funds already taken. The envelope's fourth word is the value the message
  must arrive with; ops 6 (OPEN) and 7 (MOVE);
- **`open_token`** — asks the hub for a token this chain was left out of.
  The opener pays the message and the hub's SYNC; the replica's rent comes
  WITH that SYNC (below);
- **the hub's messages bring what they cost** — a SYNC and the PAYOUT of a
  moved claim arrive with one `returnUnit` of lamports (set on the hub
  adapter for label 1: **3,000,000**, §9). An account such a message
  creates is paid by those lamports, then by the tank, then — only then —
  by whoever executes it; what the message did not need goes to the tank;
- **`move_claim`** — a claim leaves for another chain at that chain's own
  price (`set_move_legs`, the operator's); never paused;
- **a PAYOUT for any 32-byte recipient** — a claim moved HERE names a
  recipient typed on another chain: it is paid, or booked in a user account
  opened on the spot (§2 row 13). The hub forwards only a key whose top
  twelve bytes are not all zero: an EVM address pasted by mistake goes home;
- **CCTP out** — `set_cctp_peer` (admin, once per label) and `rebalance`
  (operator) level the vault with the EVM vaults;
- **gaps closed** — the store is born paused; the executor's lookup table
  can be named after `init_store` (`set_receive_table`) and is frozen once
  full; the wiring pins both LayerZero libraries.

## 1. Where Solana differs from an EVM spoke, and why

Three facts of the platform drive every difference:

- **No try/catch.** A failed CPI takes the whole transaction down, and with
  it the hub message being delivered. So `lz_receive` checks first everything
  that could make a token CPI fail (vault balance, the token accounts being
  initialised and not frozen) and never attempts a transfer that can fail.
- **Accounts cost rent, and somebody must pay it.** LayerZero's executor
  refuses to spend its own SOL: it delivers a message with the native value
  its sender bought, and no more (§2). So `lz_receive` may not create an
  account at its caller's expense beyond the value its packet bought: what a hub message itself
  calls for (a replica, the account of a recipient who never came here) is
  paid by the lamports that message brings — the hub attaches them — and,
  failing that, by the program's tank.
- **SPL tokens have no transfer hook** (classic Token program; USDC is one).
  Nothing can observe a wallet-to-wallet transfer.

What follows from them:

| EVM spoke | Solana | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `OmniUSD` — an ERC-20 oUSD wrapping the vault's USDC | **No SPL oUSD.** A USDC vault (token account PDA `["Vault"]`, authority = the store) plus a per-user `usd_claim` in the user's account | oUSD exists on EVM so that a payout the vault cannot make becomes a transferable balance. A second mint here would need a token account per user (rent nobody can pay at delivery time) and adds nothing: nothing on Solana consumes oUSD. A claim is a number in an account the user already owns. |
| A payout that cannot be made mints oUSD | It becomes a **claim** (`redeem_claim` when the vault has USDC again, or `move_claim` to a chain that can pay it). Triggers: vault short, vault frozen, recipient's USDC account missing, frozen or not theirs — **for any 32-byte recipient** (v7.2): one who never came here gets a user account opened on the spot, with the lamports the PAYOUT brought | Same rule, without the token. An EVM spoke reverts on a recipient that is not an address (the hub filters them); here every 32 bytes are a possible key, so nothing may revert |
| `moveClaim` burns oUSD, which `wrap` can mint from USDC | `move_claim` burns the caller's **claim** only. No `wrap`: USDC in a wallet cannot leave this way | The USDT vault's rule (`OmniUSDLocal`), for the same reason: wrap + move is a free exchange through the vaults |
| A SYNC deploys a contract; a moved PAYOUT mints: gas, nothing else | A SYNC creates two accounts, a moved PAYOUT may open one: **rent**. The hub sends both with one `returnUnit` of lamports (0 on every EVM label), and the program spends them on the account, the rest going to the tank | F-24. Without it the rent fell on a tank nobody funds and anyone could spend from any chain |
| `buyTokenUsdc` / `buyTokenUsdcMin`, `sellToken` / `sellTokenMin` | One `buy_token`, one `sell_token`, **a minimum required** (`ZeroMinimum`); one unit (0.000001) says "anything" | Every trade from here is cross-chain: it executes minutes after it is public and anyone may deliver it. There is no synchronous path where an unprotected trade is safe, so there is no unprotected entry point |
| The deadline is `block.timestamp + orderTtl`, 30 minutes by default | `Clock::unix_timestamp + order_ttl`, **one hour** by default; the same bounds (2 minutes to a day) | The hub reads it against Base's clock. Solana's clock is a stake-weighted estimate — within a second of real time on an ordinary day (measured), behind it when the cluster runs slow or comes back from a halt; a clock that lags makes orders expire that much early. Thirty minutes like everyone, thirty for the clock (§2 row 16) |
| The gateway deploys the replica and mints to any address | `SYNC` creates the replica entry + SPL mint **with the lamports it brings** (failing that, out of the program's tank, `["GasTank"]`); a `DELIVER` mints into the token account the buyer created when they bought | Rent. Users pay their own accounts when they send; a `SYNC` has nobody here to charge, so whoever caused it pays on the chain where they are |
| A `DELIVER` that beats its `SYNC` is parked in the gateway | Parked **in the recipient's user account**, to the wei (max 4 tokens at once), claimed with `claim_parked` | Same rule; the user account is the one account that always exists (or is opened on the spot) |
| `FeeDistributor`: shares follow wallet balances through the transfer hook | **Stake to earn.** Replicas staked in the token's escrow earn the chain's holder-fee slice pro rata (`stake` / `unstake` / `claim_rewards`). Unstaked balances earn nothing | No hook, so wallet balances cannot be tracked. Unstaking is always open, even when paused |
| One legacy transaction per action | Sends need a **v0 transaction with an address lookup table** | A buy touches 40 accounts: 1509 bytes legacy, 553 bytes with the store's table |
| `quoteBuy()` etc. are views | `quote_send` is a simulated instruction; the send itself takes a fee **ceiling** (`max_native_fee`, 0 = none) and pays the actual fee | The ULN charges the real fee and only checks it against the ceiling. An on-chain quote before each send doubled the compute and ran out of heap with a mainnet pathway's 5 workers |

Every user-facing send (`spawn_token`, `buy_token`, `sell_token`,
`claim_fees`) creates, at the user's expense, the accounts the reply will
need: their user account, their USDC account, and for a buy their token
account. That is what lets every reply to a TRADE settle with zero lamports.
Two messages are not replies to someone who prepared accounts here: a SYNC
(a launch on any chain, an open, a resync) and the PAYOUT of a claim moved
from another chain to a recipient of the mover's choosing. Those two — the
messages the pad is free to skip — are the ones the hub sends with lamports
(`returnUnit[1]` on the hub adapter, §9): they pay for what they create.

One wrinkle that cannot be designed away: **a dev buy on a spawn from Solana
always takes a second transaction.** The creator cannot hold a token account
for a mint that does not exist yet, so the dev buy's `DELIVER` is parked and
the creator runs `claim_parked`.

Roles mirror `Roles.sol`: **admin** (two-step transfer; also the LayerZero
delegate, moved to the new admin on `accept_admin`) wires peers, names
operator and guardian, recovers the tank, names the executor's lookup table
and the CCTP peers; **operator** sets prices (fee config, open legs, move
legs per destination), the order lifetime and the deposit cap, rebalances
the vault toward a named peer, and unpauses; **guardian** pauses. A pause
blocks buys, spawns and opens, never sells, claims, pulls, moves, redemptions, unstaking or
inbound messages. **A store is born paused.**

## 2. Where the immutable hub constrains Solana

| # | Constraint (what the hub does, forever) | Status | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Options format.** The hub adapter builds `abi.encodePacked(uint16 3, uint8 1, uint16 17, uint8 1, uint128 gas)` — a type-3, single `lzReceive` option | **Solved** | It is exactly what LayerZero's Solana executor reads. Base's executor quotes it for eid 30168 today (0.00078 ETH for 300k on the default verifiers). The program builds the same bytes for its own sends; cross-checked against ethers' `solidityPacked` (`options` vectors) |
| 2 | **What a hub message brings.** A reply (DELIVER, the PAYOUT of a sale or a claim, FEE_DROP, a claim sent back) carries no value. A message the pad is free to skip — a SYNC, a moved PAYOUT — carries one `returnUnit[1]` of lamports (v7.2, final: F-24), named in the envelope and bought in the executor option | **Solved** | A reply needs no lamports (§1). A SYNC and a moved PAYOUT pay for the accounts they create out of what they bring; the rest goes to the tank. Proven through LayerZero's real executor program: with the unit set as §9 says and a tank holding nothing but its own rent floor, it delivers both and spends exactly what the packet bought; with the unit too low the tank makes up the difference while it can, then the executor refuses (`InsufficientBalance`) and the packet stays intact — executable again once the tank is topped up, the account opened by anyone, or the rent paid by whoever runs it; a delivery that brings less than the envelope names is refused |
| 3 | **`gas` means compute units** on Solana: `gasLimit[1]`, or since v7.2 one number per kind of message (`gasForOp[1][op]`), operator-set, bounded at 15,000,000 | **Solved — needs the number set** | Measured through the real executor and the real endpoint `clear`: at most ~147k CU, under 170k with the spread between runs (§5). Set `gasLimit[1] = 400_000` and leave `gasForOp` unset. Price is flat: 100k → 400k CU costs +0.000013 ETH per message. The hub's bound is far above Solana's own ceiling of 1,400,000 CU per transaction: for label 1 the tooling must stay under THAT |
| 4 | **A token's `SYNC` reaches a chain at launch, or when someone asks.** v7.2: the fan-out skips a chain above its `syncCap`; `resync` (anyone, paid on the hub) and `OP_OPEN` (from the chain itself) send it later | **Solved** | A `SYNC` is never lost: it brings its rent (row 2); if it brings too little and the tank cannot make it up it waits, executable by the executor once the tank is topped up or by anyone willing to pay 0.0027 SOL. A second `SYNC` is ignored. A token Solana never got — skipped, failed, or older than the leg — is opened from here: `open_token` prepays the hub's SYNC, which brings its own rent |
| 5 | **Every launch on every chain sends a `SYNC` to Solana, synchronously** (`_spawn` loops `messenger.send`). If that send reverts, the launch reverts | **Blocking until the hub-side config is in the same batch** | LayerZero's default executor config for Base → Solana admits **200-byte** messages (read from Base mainnet; `endpoint.quote` reverts `LZ_MessageLib_InvalidMessageSize(384, 200)`). Hub messages are 288–480 bytes. The adapter's executor config for eid 30168 must be raised to 10,000 **before** `pad.setGateway(1, …)` executes, in the same atomic timelock batch (§7) — otherwise every launch on Base, Optimism and Arbitrum reverts until a second 48h operation fixes it |
| 6 | **Message size vs Solana's 1232-byte transaction.** The largest hub message (a `SYNC` with a 64-byte name and 16-byte symbol) is 512 bytes (v7.2: each message is one word longer) | **Solved locally, unproven live** | The executor transaction is 803–1038 bytes for all four messages with a lookup table of the static accounts (measured). Whether LayerZero's off-chain executor builds it that way is only provable on a live cluster |
| 7 | **Supply accounting (SPEC F-4).** The hub counts label 1's supply in 18 decimals; SPL mints have 6 | **Bounded: under 1e12 wei (one millionth of a token) per token, ever** | Each replica carries the unminted remainder (`dust_carry`) into the next delivery: Σ minted × 1e12 + carry = Σ delivered, exactly. Sells burn whole SPL units and send `units × 1e12`: always an exact multiple, so the hub is never asked for dust it does not think label 1 has. Parked deliveries are kept to the wei |
| 8 | **Dollar amounts are 18-decimal** on the wire; USDC has 6 | **Bounded: under 0.000001 USDC per message** | Payouts round down; the remainder stays in the vault and is counted (`store.usd_dust_e18`). Deposits are exact (`usdc × 1e12`) |
| 9 | **Holder fees are split by chain supply**, staked or not, and reach a chain only when someone there pulls them | **Bounded — a disclosed difference** | `pull_fees` (op 5) brings the slice; stakers share it. If fewer than 1,000 tokens are staked the slice waits in `pending_fees` and goes to the first stakers to reach that floor. Holders who do not stake earn nothing on Solana |
| 10 | **Addressing.** Token ids, actors and gateways are `bytes32` on the wire; the envelope is `abi.encode(bytes32 srcSender, bytes32 dstAddr, bytes payload, uint256 value)` (v7.2) | **Solved** | Solana keys ride as they are. The program accepts a message only from the hub eid, from the LayerZero peer (the hub adapter), with `srcSender` = the pad and `dstAddr` = this store. A three-field (pre-v7.2) envelope is refused, not half-read |
| 11 | **Replies are prepaid in the hub's currency, and the hub adapter insists on it (v7.2).** The user's send carries `return_unit × legs` of Base ETH into the hub adapter's tank; the same number rides the envelope, and a delivery that brings less is refused | **Solved, at a price — and under a cap that is LayerZero's** | Solana's executor prices it: a 0.0002 ETH reply budget costs 0.0058 SOL today. A reply to Solana costs the hub ≈ 0.00028 ETH on our verifier set, so `return_unit` here should be ≈ 0.00035 ETH → **a buy from Solana costs ≈ 0.0115 SOL** in messaging. LayerZero's Solana executor admits at most **0.05 ETH of value per message toward Base** (its config, mainnet snapshot): `return_unit × legs` above it and the send is refused whole (`NativeAmountExceedsCap`, tested at 250 / 251 legs of 0.0002 ETH). `set_move_legs` allows 4,096 legs; the operator must price a dear destination under the cap |
| 12 | **`pad.setGateway` is permanent.** There is no way to remove a chain | **A decision, not a defect** | After the batch executes, every launch whose fan-out reaches Solana pays one more `SYNC` — about twice a plain reply, because it carries the replica's rent (≈ 0.00057 ETH on our verifier set, §5; prepaid by the launcher). `syncCap[1]` (operator, reversible) takes Solana out of the fan-out: tokens are then opened from here, at the opener's expense |
| 13 | **A recipient someone chose (v7.2).** `OP_MOVE` makes the hub forward a PAYOUT to a 32-byte `to` on a non-EVM gateway — any key whose top twelve bytes are not all zero (F-25: an address-shaped one goes home) | **Solved** | No recipient can make a PAYOUT fail or its account list unresolvable (§3). USDC if the vault and the recipient's own USDC account allow it, else a claim in the recipient's user account — opened on the spot for one who never came here, with the lamports the PAYOUT brought (0.0019 SOL of the unit). A recipient nobody holds the key of (a token account's address, a program) gets a claim nobody can redeem — the sender's mistake, as with any transfer |
| 14 | **A claim moved OUT is paid where the hub can hand it on, else comes home.** `OP_MOVE` is forwarded only under `requestCap[dst][12]`; the way home is a reply | **Solved** | `move_claim` burns the claim first; if it comes home the PAYOUT names `from`, whose user account exists (it held the claim). The move prepays `max(move legs of that destination, trade legs)` |
| 15 | **A trade under its minimum — or after its deadline — is refused whole (v7.2).** A buy's dollars come home in a `DELIVER`'s refund; a sold bag is delivered back | **Solved** | Both are ordinary `DELIVER`s for the trader who sent: refund as USDC or a claim, bag minted or parked. The minimum is stated in local units and scaled ×1e12, so "at least N units" on the hub is at least N here |
| 16 | **Two clocks (v7.2, final).** A trade's fifth word is its deadline in unix seconds; the gateway writes it from ITS clock and the hub reads it against Base's. The hub decodes five words: a shorter trade reverts for good | **Solved; bounded by the lifetime** | The program stamps `Clock::unix_timestamp + order_ttl` on every BUY and SELL — never 0 — and no caller chooses it. Solana's clock is the stake-weighted median of validators' vote times, held within 25% fast / 150% slow of nominal slot time since the epoch began (Agave, `stake_weighted_timestamp.rs`): **0–1 s behind real time on mainnet and devnet today (measured)**; behind by minutes when the cluster averages over a second per slot or returns from a halt early in an epoch (hours, in the worst case, shrinking at 1.5 s per second). Lag `L` shortens every order's life by `L`: over the lifetime, every order bounces whole (a fee and a retry, never a loss) until the clock catches up or the operator lengthens `order_ttl` (a day at most). Default **one hour**: the EVM gateways' thirty minutes plus thirty for this clock — the largest sustained drift I know of on mainnet was about half an hour (2022, under older, tighter bounds: from memory, not re-verified) |

## 3. Implemented and proven

`anchor build`, `cargo test` and `npm run client:test` are green: **41 Rust
tests** (9 codec, 17 on the handlers, 15 against the real stack) and **61
client tests**. The real stack is the bytecode of LayerZero's mainnet
Endpoint, ULN 302, Executor, PriceFeed and five DVN programs, their mainnet
state for the Solana ↔ Base pathway, the real USDC mint, and Circle's CCTP
V2 MessageTransmitter and TokenMessengerMinter with their mainnet state,
loaded into LiteSVM (`tests/fetch-fixtures.sh mainnet`).

Wire (`src/msg_codec.rs`, `app/src/wire.ts`): ops 1–7 out, 10–13 in, the
envelope, and the option bytes — both codecs held, byte for byte and both
ways, to `tests/codec-vectors-v72.gen.json`: **34 op vectors, 4 envelopes
and 4 option vectors generated from the final contracts' coder**
(`contracts/scripts/gen-codec-vectors.js` → `lib-wire.js`). The
hand-written `tests/codec-vectors-v72.json` (6 ops, 4 envelopes, word by
word) is kept as a second, annotated witness: the generator itself holds it
to the coder and exits 1 on a difference, and both suites hold their codec
to it. The old `tests/codec-vectors.json` is kept for one thing: its
three-word trades and three-field envelopes are asserted REFUSED, as is the
four-word trade of v7.2's second round.

v7.2, against the real programs (`tests/test_live_endpoint.rs`,
`app/test/*.test.ts`):

- **every send names its reply budget in the envelope** — the same number
  as in the executor option — and every trade its minimum;
- **every buy and sell leaves with five words**, the fifth a deadline
  stamped from the cluster clock: it follows the clock (not the client's
  guess of it), follows `set_order_ttl`, is never 0 even on a cluster
  whose clock reads 0, and only a trade has one;
- **`open_token`**: OP_OPEN through the real endpoint, `open_legs` prepaid
  and no rent charged; the hub's SYNC then lands through LayerZero's real
  executor WITH its unit of lamports: the message pays for the replica,
  the executor spends exactly what the packet bought, the rest is the
  tank's; a second SYNC is ignored and leaves its whole unit in the tank;
  refused when the replica exists, for a wide id, when paused; a bag
  parked behind a missing replica is claimed after;
- **`move_claim`**: OP_MOVE through the real endpoint at the destination's
  price, never below a trade's legs; refused for this chain, the hub's
  label, a zero recipient, an unpriced or closed route, more than the
  claim, someone else's claim; passes when paused; comes home as a PAYOUT;
  refused whole above LayerZero's 0.05 ETH cap;
- **a moved claim arriving** for a recipient the chain never saw — a fresh
  key, another program's address, the all-zero and all-ones keys, the
  store, the vault, the tank, the USDC mint, a sysvar, this program, the
  executor itself: delivered by LayerZero's real executor with the unit it
  brings, the account paid by the message. With the unit set too low the
  tank covers the difference while it holds a float, then the executor
  refuses and the same packet lands once the account is opened or the tank
  topped up; with no unit at all it is the tank's, as before;
- **`rebalance`** through Circle's real CCTP V2 programs: the USDC is
  burned, Circle's message names the peer vault, Solana (5) → Base (6);
  not the operator, no peer, a wrong domain, a program that is not
  Circle's: refused;
- **the wiring** pins both libraries on the real endpoint, freezes the
  table, sets every route and peer, and a re-run sends nothing.

Against the real LayerZero programs (`tests/test_live_endpoint.rs`):

- registration with the real endpoint; a new admin becomes the delegate;
- **users pay**: `spawn_token`, `buy_token`, `sell_token`, `claim_fees`,
  `pull_fees` each send through endpoint → ULN → executor → price feed → DVNs,
  the user's own lamports pay the fee, the packet carries our bytes and the
  reply budget; a ceiling below the fee is refused by the ULN; a sponsored
  store (testnets) refunds from the tank and stops when it is empty;
- **entry checks** refuse before any money moves: fee mode > 3, name > 64
  bytes, symbol > 16, zero amounts, an unknown token, a deposit over the cap,
  a paused store (sells, claims and pulls still pass), a fee payer that is
  not the user. The 1024-byte metadata limit is in the program but cannot be
  reached: a spawn must fit one 1232-byte transaction, which leaves roughly
  700 bytes for the metadata URI;
- **the real executor delivers every reply with zero value** (and a SYNC
  the hub sent with none, out of the tank): `execute` → our `lz_receive` →
  the real `clear`, with the account lists our `lz_receive_types_v2`
  returns (a pure function of the message: it reads no account, so nothing
  about a recipient can change or break it); replay fails; out-of-order
  nonces work; a `SYNC` that brought nothing to a dry tank is refused by
  the executor and executed by a volunteer;
- a send fits one v0 transaction with a lookup table;
- **our verifier set** — LayerZero Labs + Google required, 1 of Horizen /
  Twinstake — set by the delegate on the real endpoint and ULN, and a buy
  sent over it (2.7× cheaper than the default set);
- every hub message fits one executor transaction.

On the handlers (`tests/test_gateway.rs`): an account a message creates is
paid by what the message brought, then by the tank (what was brought
joining it first), then by the caller — each of the three for a replica and
for a recipient's account, and the event says which; `SYNC` duplicate
ignored, pre-funded addresses do not block it; `DELIVER` exact to the wei
with the carry, with a USDC refund; `DELIVER` before `SYNC`, or to a missing
or frozen token account, parked then claimed; parking full is a retryable
failure; `PAYOUT` in USDC, or a claim when the vault is short or frozen or
the recipient's account is unusable, then `redeem_claim`; a `PAYOUT` for
thirteen kinds of recipient that are not a user of this chain; a value
named by the envelope taken from the caller, what the message did not need
left in the tank, a delivery that brings less than it names refused; the
fee pool
(waiting below the floor, pro rata, late stakers earn only from when they
stake, unstake, claim paid as a claim when the vault is short); `FEE_DROP`
for an unknown token fails and stays retryable; only the pad through the
adapter is heard (wrong peer, forged envelope, wrong target, wrong
recipient, a spoke op, garbage); the three roles and the pause, with the
bounds each setter holds (an order lifetime of two minutes to a day, at
most 15,000,000 of gas bought on the hub, at most 128 spawn legs); the
vault is named once and only over a 6-decimal mint.

**What no local test proves:** DVNs actually signing, LayerZero's off-chain
executor resolving our accounts and landing the transaction, and anything on
the Base side of the pathway.

## 4. Designed, not built

- **Token metadata.** Replicas have no Metaplex metadata account, so wallets
  show an unnamed token. The name and symbol are stored in the replica entry
  (the hub's SYNC carries them; it does not carry the metadata URI, which
  stays on the hub). A permissionless `init_metadata` paid by whoever calls
  it is the plan, its URI derived from the hub token id and served by the
  site.
- **A timelock on the admin.** The EVM side waits 48h in public before any
  topology change; this program has no delay of its own. The admin and the
  program's upgrade authority should be a Squads multisig with a time lock.
  Since v7.2 the admin also names CCTP peers — a way out of the vault.
- **Buying with a claim, selling straight from a stake.** Today: redeem, then
  buy; unstake, then sell.
- **A relayer for CCTP.** `rebalance` burns; someone must take Circle's
  attestation to the destination chain (`destinationCaller` is nobody: anyone
  can). Manual on the EVM side too. Circle's message account (428 bytes,
  0.0028 SOL, paid by the operator) can be reclaimed by its payer after
  Circle's window — no code here does it.
- **Executor gas per kind of message.** The EVM adapters got `gasForOp` in
  v7.2 (a buy toward the hub buys 800,000 gas, a spawn 5.2M or more); this
  program still buys one `gas_to_hub` for every message it sends, sized for
  the spawn. Measured: a million of gas on Base costs a send 144,632
  lamports, so a trade from Solana overpays about 0.00075 SOL — a tenth of
  its messaging cost on our verifier set. A setter per op is an upgrade
  away, and nothing on the hub depends on it.
- **In the site.** Nothing yet. `app/` is the client; `scripts/cli.ts` drives
  it for the rehearsal.

Built since the v7 port: the outbound CCTP leg (`set_cctp_peer`,
`rebalance`); `open_token`, `move_claim`; the client and the wiring (`app/`,
`scripts/wire.ts`), the rehearsal command line (`scripts/cli.ts`); and for
the final wire the deadline (`set_order_ttl`) and the funding of what a hub
message creates by the lamports it brings.

## 8. What stands between this program and real money

In order:

1. **A live rehearsal.** Nothing here has met a real DVN or LayerZero's real
   off-chain executor. Devnet ↔ Base Sepolia proves the pathway, the account
   resolution, the 300–480-byte deliveries and the 400k CU figure.
2. **The EVM tooling for a non-EVM spoke.** `add-chain.sh`, `tune-fees.js`
   and the profiles keep label 1 reserved and know nothing else about it:
   the hub-side calls of §7 (the unit in lamports, the gas in compute
   units, the ceilings, the legs) are to be made by hand or written there.
   And the site: the client (`app/`) and the command line exist and are
   tested; nothing in `src/` uses them yet.
3. **The hub batch, with the executor config in it** (§2 row 5). Getting the
   order wrong stops every launch on every chain for 48h.
4. **The hub's unit for this chain, kept in step with Solana's rent**
   (§9). Set too low, SYNCs draw on the tank's float and then wait. The
   tank's balance and `solana rent` are the two ops alerts.
5. **Custody**: who holds the upgrade authority and the admin. With a hot key
   on either, that key can take the vault (the admin, since v7.2, also by
   naming a CCTP peer).
6. **An independent review.** ~3,200 lines of Rust hold a USDC vault and
   have been read only by their authors. The EVM side launched unaudited as
   a decision; this deserves the same explicit decision, not a default.
7. **Disclosures on the site**: stake to earn, the second transaction after a
   dev buy, a token not being on Solana until it is opened (and what that
   costs), a minimum on every trade, an order that expires after an hour
   and comes back whole (the messaging fee is spent), metadata, and — for
   a move TO Solana — that the recipient is a WALLET address (a Solana
   key), never a token account: the claim would be booked for a key nobody
   holds. (An EVM address is sent home by the hub.)

## 9. What the final hub gives Solana, what its operator must set, what still does not fit

Both things this file asked of the hub before it was frozen are in the
final contracts (SPEC F-24, F-25), and the deadline of its last round
(F-26) is carried here.

### The unit: `returnUnit` of label 1 on the HUB adapter = 3,000,000 lamports

The pad passes one return leg on the messages it is free to skip — a SYNC
(at a launch, for an open, for a resync) and the PAYOUT a move forwards —
and the hub adapter attaches `returnUnit[1]` to them: lamports, delivered by
LayerZero's executor with the message and named in the envelope. It is the
operator's (`setDestination(1, gas, unit)`), so it can follow Solana's rent.

- **What it must cover.** The dearer of the two things such a message can
  create here: a replica's two accounts, (128 + 201) × 5,080 + (128 + 82) ×
  5,080 = **2,738,120 lamports** at today's rent on mainnet and devnet. (The
  other, a recipient's user account, is 1,894,840.)
- **What to set: 3,000,000** — that rent plus about a tenth, rounded
  (`suggestedHubUnit`; `npm run cli -- status` and the wiring print the
  figure for the cluster they run against). The tenth is for the rent
  moving before the operator notices, and because every lamport a message
  does not need goes to this program's tank: 261,880 a SYNC, 1,105,160 a
  moved PAYOUT that opens an account, all 3,000,000 one that does not. That
  float is what covers a unit that has fallen behind, and the rare DELIVER
  that has to open an account.
- **What it costs.** LayerZero charges ≈ 0.000285 ETH on Base to deliver
  3,000,000 lamports (measured, §5): a SYNC or a moved PAYOUT toward Solana
  is about twice a plain reply, ≈ 0.00057 ETH on our verifier set. Whoever
  causes it prepays it through the existing prices — which must therefore
  be sized on it: `requestCap(1, 10)`, `requestCap(1, 12)`, `syncCap[1]`,
  `moveLegs(1)` on every gateway, one more leg in every `spawnLegs`, and
  here `open_legs` = 2 and one more `spawn_legs`.
- **Set too low** (under the rent of what a message creates): the message's
  lamports join the tank and the tank pays the account; the executor still
  spends only what the packet bought. Each such SYNC costs the tank the
  difference. When the tank cannot cover it, LayerZero's executor declines
  the delivery (its own balance check) and the packet waits, verified:
  it lands when the tank is topped up (a plain transfer, by anyone), when
  anyone opens the missing account, or when anyone runs it and pays the
  rent. Nothing is lost; that token is not on Solana, or that claim not
  booked, until then. **Set to 0**: the same, from the first message.
- **Set too high**: the surplus piles up in the tank (the admin's to
  recover) and every launch and every move toward Solana overpays. Above
  240,000,000 (LayerZero's cap toward Solana, 0.24 SOL) the hub's quote
  fails: SYNCs are skipped (`SyncFailed`) and moves come home. **The unit is
  in lamports, nine decimals**: the hub's own bound (`MAX_RETURN_UNIT`,
  0.05 "ether" = 5e16) does not catch a figure typed in wei.
- **A reply brings nothing**, and needs nothing: its recipient opened their
  accounts when they sent.

### The order lifetime: one hour

`order_ttl` is the operator's, two minutes to a day, like every gateway's;
a store is born with 3,600 s where an EVM gateway has 1,800. The half-hour
more is for the clock (§2 row 16): a deadline written from a clock that
lags is that much shorter on the hub. It costs a stuck order thirty more
minutes of exposure at its minimum — and on this chain every order names a
minimum. An hour does not absorb the lag that follows a cluster halt
(hours): orders then bounce whole until the clock catches up, or the
operator lengthens the lifetime for the while. The tooling refuses a
lifetime under ten minutes: the pathway's ordinary two would bounce on it.
